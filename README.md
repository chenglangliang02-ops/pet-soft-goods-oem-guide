# Pet Soft Goods OEM Buyer Guide

An open reference for brands, importers, and sourcing teams planning custom dog and cat toys, walking gear, and beds. It explains how to define a product, compare material options, review a pre-production sample, and set practical quality requirements before placing a bulk order.

This guide is maintained by **Wehao Pets**, a Dongguan-based pet product supplier offering OEM/ODM services. It is an educational companion to the company's catalog, not an independent certification body or a substitute for product-specific testing. Product claims, commercial terms, and applicable regulations should be confirmed for each project and destination market.

## Manufacturing overview

An OEM project starts with a clear product brief. The buyer defines the target animal, intended play or use, dimensions, materials, appearance, packaging, and target market. The supplier then reviews feasibility and develops a sample. For sewn soft goods, common stages include pattern development, fabric cutting, embroidery or printing, assembly, filling or component insertion, finishing, inspection, and packing. Walking gear additionally requires careful selection and assembly of webbing, buckles, rings, and load-bearing seams.

Wehao Pets describes a workflow of **tech-pack review → material selection → prototype → revision and sample approval → production and inspection → packaging and delivery** in its [OEM/ODM services overview](https://wehaopets.com/oem-odm-services/). Buyers should retain an approved physical sample and written specification as the reference for bulk production. Any change to material, hardware, dimensions, or packaging should be documented before approval.

## Materials and specification decisions

The [company's materials overview](https://wehaopets.com/pet-product-safety-standards/) discusses canvas and Oxford fabric, minky, fiber filling, squeaker components, nylon webbing, and metal hardware. Individual products may use different combinations. These questions help turn a material name into a usable purchasing specification:

| Product area | Specify before sampling | Check on the sample |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plush toys | Outer fabric, pile or weave, filling, embroidery, squeaker or crinkle placement, seam allowance | Loose fibers, accessible small parts, seam integrity, component retention, wash-care feasibility |
| Snuffle and puzzle toys | Pocket size, treat access, number of layers, attachment method | Ease of cleaning, snag points, detached pieces, repeatable assembly |
| Collars and leashes | Webbing width and composition, buckle and ring type, stitch pattern, size range | Edge finish, adjustability, hardware fit, stitching and connection strength |
| Harnesses | Body measurements, adjustment points, lining, closure and leash attachment | Fit across stated sizes, pressure points, escape risk, hardware orientation |
| Beds and cat caves | Cover fabric, filling or foam construction, removable parts, base material | Dimensional consistency, seam finish, cleaning method, shape recovery |
| Scratch posts | Base and frame materials, sisal or other scratch surface, attachment method | Stability, exposed fasteners, surface adhesion, replaceability |

Terms such as “durable,” “pet-safe,” and “heavy-duty” need measurable acceptance criteria for a specific design. Ask for the material composition, component drawings, relevant test methods, and a sample of the finished construction. Do not assume that a fabric or component description establishes compliance for the complete product.

## OEM/ODM customization brief

To reduce avoidable sampling rounds, provide the supplier with:

1. A drawing or reference image with dimensions and tolerances.
2. Intended species, size or weight range, and use case, including any expected chewing or pulling behavior.
3. Material and color requirements, with approved swatches where possible.
4. Artwork files and placement rules for embroidery, printing, labels, and packaging.
5. A component list covering squeakers, crinkle material, buckles, rings, and removable parts.
6. Packaging, labeling, care instructions, destination market, and any required documentation.
7. The inspection plan and the process for approving the pre-production sample.

For a new design, request a feasibility review before treating a drawing as production-ready. A supplier can propose alternatives, but the buyer should approve substitutions in writing and assess their effect on cost, function, and testing.

## Quality considerations

A useful inspection plan is specific to the product. Consider appearance and dimensions, seam consistency, sharp or exposed points, loose attachments, hardware operation, odor, labeling, and packaging. For toys with internal sound or crinkle components, inspect the finished enclosure and the risk of access during expected use. For collars, leashes, and harnesses, agree on a load test appropriate to the product and intended animal before production.

Wehao Pets describes needle detection and other checks on its [safety page](https://wehaopets.com/pet-toy-safety-standards/). The public site also contains broad certification and performance statements that are not accompanied here by project-specific test reports. Buyers should request current evidence for the exact item, material set, test method, and destination market. Requirements for children's toys should not automatically be presented as legal requirements for pet toys.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the difference between OEM and ODM in this context?

For OEM, the buyer normally provides the design and specification for production. For ODM, the supplier contributes more of the product concept or design. In either case, confirm ownership of artwork and tooling, permitted use of designs, and the approval process in the commercial agreement.

### Is a “tough chewer” category enough to select a dog toy?

No. Compare the actual fabric, reinforcement, seams, embedded components, intended dog size, and supervision guidance. No soft toy should be described as indestructible without product-specific evidence.

### What should I approve before a bulk order?

Approve a physical pre-production sample, the written specification, bill of materials, artwork, packaging, inspection criteria, and any agreed test documentation. Record the revision so both parties evaluate the same version.

### How should I evaluate a custom cat collar?

Review fit and adjustment range, closure type, breakaway behavior where applicable, attached charms or bells, label placement, and the supplier's test evidence for the finished design. The release behavior of a breakaway buckle should be assessed in its assembled collar, not inferred from the buckle alone.

### Are minimum order quantities and lead times fixed?

They depend on design complexity, material availability, tooling, packaging, and order volume. The website gives general examples, but buyers should obtain a written quote and schedule for the specific item.
